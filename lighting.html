Common questions about lighting work

Answers to what Colorado Springs homeowners ask most.

How much does ceiling fan installation cost in Colorado Springs?
Ceiling fan installs are quoted flat rate once we see the job. The price depends on whether you already have a ceiling box rated for a fan (most light-fixture boxes aren't), whether new wiring is needed, and ceiling height. We won't give you a number that changes after we start — call or text and we'll give you a straight quote.
Do I need an electrician to install a dimmer switch?
Technically you can do it yourself, but there are a few common mistakes: choosing a dimmer that's not compatible with your specific LED bulbs (most dimmers were designed for incandescent loads), missing the neutral wire that modern smart dimmers require, and not sizing the dimmer for multi-bulb circuits. If the switch controls multiple fixtures or you're upgrading to a smart dimmer, it's worth having it done right the first time.
Can you add recessed lighting to an existing room?
Yes — retrofit recessed cans are designed exactly for this. We run the circuit, cut the holes evenly spaced, patch any drywall disturbed, and connect everything back to the switch. Most finished-room recessed lighting projects in Colorado Springs take one visit. We'll tell you upfront if your ceiling situation (insulation, joists, HVAC) makes it more involved.
What kind of outdoor lighting can you install?
Soffit lights, motion-activated security lighting, landscape path lights (low-voltage or line-voltage), garage lights, and GFCI-protected exterior outlets. All outdoor electrical work in Colorado Springs needs to be weather-rated and code-correct — we pull permits when required and make sure the work holds up to Colorado's freeze-thaw cycles.
Why do my LED bulbs flicker or buzz with my current dimmer?
Most older dimmers were designed for incandescent bulbs. LEDs draw far less power and behave differently on the same dimming circuits — you'll often get buzzing, flickering, or a limited dimming range. Replacing the dimmer with an LED-rated model (or a smart dimmer with a correct neutral) almost always fixes this. We carry compatible replacements and can swap it same visit.
How do I know if my ceiling box can support a ceiling fan?
A standard ceiling box for a light fixture is not rated to hold a fan's weight or handle the constant motion. Fans require a fan-rated box — either an old-work brace kit installed from below (no attic access needed) or a properly mounted fan box from above. We check and replace the box as part of every ceiling fan installation so it's safe long-term.
